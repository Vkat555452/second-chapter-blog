eHarmony vs. Hinge: Which Fits Serious Daters?

Both call themselves apps for people who want a relationship. Here's how they actually differ once you're using them.

By the Second Chapter team • 7 min read • Updated 2026
Disclosure: This post contains an affiliate link for eHarmony. If you sign up through it, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. We don't have an affiliate relationship with Hinge, and that has no bearing on this comparison — see our full disclosure policy.

"For serious daters" has become a marketing line almost every app uses now. eHarmony and Hinge both genuinely lean that direction more than swipe-first apps do, but they get there in very different ways — and which one suits you often comes down to how much structure you actually want before you start talking to someone.

The core difference: questionnaire-first vs. profile-first

eHarmony asks you to complete a detailed compatibility questionnaire before you see anyone — covering values, personality traits, and what you're looking for. It then sends you a limited set of matches based on that.

Hinge skips the long questionnaire. You build a profile with photos and prompts (short written answers meant to spark conversation), and you browse other profiles directly, sending a "like" on a specific photo or prompt rather than swiping blind.

If you want eHarmony's approach

  • You're tired of deciding who's "worth a shot" from a photo alone
  • You'd rather answer some questions upfront than build a profile from scratch
  • You're looking for a smaller, more curated set of matches over a big pool

If you want Hinge's approach

  • You want to see and choose people yourself, not be matched algorithmically
  • You like expressing personality through profile prompts and photos
  • You want a bigger, more active pool to browse, especially in cities with younger user bases

Who tends to be on each app

eHarmony's user base skews somewhat older and has historically leaned toward people specifically looking for marriage or long-term partnership — its whole model is built around that intent. Hinge markets itself as "designed to be deleted" (meant for relationships, not endless swiping) but its user base skews younger overall, particularly in urban areas, and includes people at a wider range of relationship-readiness.

Messaging and conversation style

Hinge's prompt-based likes tend to make the first message easier — you're usually responding to something specific the person wrote, rather than opening cold. eHarmony's structure means your first real interaction often comes after both the questionnaire and an initial match notification, which can feel slower but more deliberate.

Pricing

Both apps offer a free tier with paid subscription options that unlock full messaging and additional features. Exact pricing and current promotions change periodically on both platforms, so we'd rather point you to their current pricing pages than quote figures here that may be out of date.

Our honest take

Neither app is objectively "better" — they solve different frustrations. If swiping through endless profiles feels exhausting and you'd rather be matched based on real compatibility questions, eHarmony's structure does that job well. If you'd rather see people yourself and use photos and prompts to get a feel for personality before matching, Hinge's browsing model tends to feel more natural.

Some of our readers have had good results using both at once during the first month or two, simply to see which style of matching actually produces conversations that go somewhere for them.
